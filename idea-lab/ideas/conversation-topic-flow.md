# How Conversation Topics Flow

## Core Question

Across different conversation contexts — eating with family, chatting with Claude, casual talk with friends — how do **topics move**? What is the underlying structure?

Three contexts, same question:

| Context | Dynamics | 
|---------|----------|
| Family dinner | Multi-party, unfocused, topics drift by association |
| Claude dialogue | Two-party, focused, topics branch deliberately |
| Casual friend chat | Two-party, semi-focused, topics oscillate between depth and small talk |

## What "Structure" Might Mean

- Topic transition rules — what follows what?
- Depth gradients — when does a conversation go deep vs. stay shallow?
- Topic lifecycle — how is a topic introduced, developed, exhausted, abandoned?
- Role of silence / pauses — do they trigger topic switches?
- Who drives the flow — speaker-initiated vs. listener-prompted transitions?

## Possible Angles

- Flow-based thinking (Source→Router→Sink) applied to topic flow
- The (M, A) project model applied to conversation state
- Existing linguistic frameworks (conversation analysis, discourse analysis)
