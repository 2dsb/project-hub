# The Structure of Government

## Entities

As a "purposeful system," its entities can be divided into three levels from top to bottom:
1. **Government**
2. **Government departments**: the Senate
3. **Government officials**: senators, speakers, police officers, etc.

Entities related to government include: **individuals, corporations**, etc.
Special entity: **the Constitution**

Special terms: **power** and **duty**

## Attributes

Government, government departments, government officials, individuals, and corporations all have **power** and **duty**.
(From an economic perspective), the government has **funds**.

## Relationships Between Entities

1. The Constitution and other entities:
   The Constitution defines the powers and duties of all other entities — government, government officials, government departments, individuals, corporations, etc.
2. The relationship among government, government departments, and government officials:
   Government departments are parts of the government; the government is the whole of its departments (part-whole relationship).
   Government officials belong to government departments and to the government (membership and containment relationship).

## The Relationship Between Power and Duty

Power and duty correspond one-to-one; duty and power arise simultaneously.

(Economic dimension):
1. Individuals and corporations pay taxes to the government; the government collects taxes from individuals and corporations.
2. Individuals hold the power to be free from infringement of property rights; the government holds the duty to protect property rights from infringement.

## Entity Behavior

**The government can grant powers and duties to government officials, and can also revoke them.**
(Often, power and duty are the same thing.)
For example, when the government orders the military to go to war abroad, the general is granted both the power to lead the troops abroad and the duty to carry out the order.
