# Modern Technology's Defining Trait: Biological Nature

In the digital age, everything enters the digital realm and becomes the same type of operable object. This allows everything to converse with everything else, broadening the possibilities of combination.

Technology at this stage is no longer a fixed, machine-implemented functional pattern; it is a system, a functional network, a "thing-executes-thing" network — a pattern of sensing the environment and responding to it.

This kind of technology has the ability to interact with its environment, cognitive capabilities, self-repair, and self-integration. In a word, modern technology has a **biological nature**.

## How to Understand Biological Nature?

One understanding is that any technology **has both a mechanical nature and a biological nature**.

- From the top down, the mechanical nature of technology is reflected in how components are arranged and constrained to achieve a purpose.
- From the bottom up, the biological nature of technology is reflected in the holistic structure formed by its components.

Another understanding is:

- The biological nature of technology lies in its capabilities (ability to interact with the environment, cognitive abilities, etc.).
- The mechanical nature of technology lies in its interconnected functional parts.

# Under Technology's Influence, the Economy Is Becoming "Biological"

In the digital age, technology has a "biological nature," and the economy, as the expression of technology, also reflects "biological" elements.

Today's economy is not focused on perfecting fixed operations but on creating new combinations and new configurable products.

The economy is becoming **reproductive**.

In such an economy, competitive advantage comes not from reserves of resources and the ability to transform them into final products, but from the process of translating deep knowledge reserves into new strategic combinations.

# The Biological Nature of Technology Sparks a New "Way of Interpreting the World"

In the past, mechanization-dominated technology led people to form a worldview: **the world is partially and reasonably composed, and its components are governed by rationality and simplicity**. This worldview gave rise to rigid dreams of pursuing pure order.

Today, the view that replaces it is: **the world is open, evolving, an organic whole, rather than pure order**.

# How Humans View Technology

## The Nature of Technology

1. Technology exploits phenomena, turning nature into a potential resource.
2. Technology also has a need for "its own development" — it is an end in itself.
3. The essence of technology lies in the exploitation of natural phenomena, yet it feels unnatural.

## Human Needs

1. We place our hopes in technology to bring us a better life.
2. We desire oneness with nature.

## The Relationship Between Humans and Technology

The three traits of technology interact with and match against human needs.

On the one hand, technology creates wealth and security.
On the other hand, technology provokes our unease:
1. We place our hopes in technology for a better life, but we trust nature — yet technology feels unnatural, creating a conflict between hope and trust;
2. Technology is an end in itself and possesses a "biological nature" — we fear it slipping out of our control and separating us from nature;
