# Liberal Arts Education

Liberal arts education pursues knowledge for its own sake, spanning both natural sciences and humanities. What it investigates is the **process of knowledge production**. For science and engineering, it may involve analyzing assumptions within a field before proceeding further; for the humanities, it involves critical reflection on both old and new knowledge: gathering evidence, analyzing authoritative arguments, encountering counterarguments, synthesizing new viewpoints, and then rigorously articulating one's own position. It demands both the ability to analyze others' views and the ability to express one's own.

Whatever the field, frontier discoveries require feeling one's way forward. Only by advancing step by step can clarity emerge. Liberal arts education is also a process of embracing this kind of **knowledge production**.

This "process of knowledge production" spans all domains, and (therefore) the ultimate goal of liberal arts education is the ability to think beyond the constraints of space and time, unrestricted by the nature of one's work.

When it comes to thinking, we must consider its effects:
1. For others: public interest and the rights of others
2. For oneself: building a healthy, creative, and free self

In this process, one inevitably draws wisdom from predecessors — hence the need for the humanities and arts. This includes history, philosophy, religious studies, literature, and other art forms. The themes they discuss touch on everything that concerns us.

# How Does Art Lead to Truth?

Take painting as an example. Before painting, one must first observe the subject with full attention. Only after thoroughly grasping its characteristics can one put brush to paper with confidence. Literature, similarly, takes human psychology and social phenomena as its primary objects of observation — poets focus on subjective feeling, while novelists focus on authentic interactions between people.

Art helps us discover and break through these (invisible) constraints, setting us free.

Truth is hard to bear, so we deceive ourselves and desperately evade it — which in turn makes it even harder to discover truth. That is why, to break through, art is necessary.

# The Difference Between Art and Science

Scientists typically use the most objective language — data is their natural tongue.
Artists recount personal experience, aiming to evoke resonance in others. We can only interpret art; we cannot calculate it.

For a scientific phenomenon, we might ask, "Is it true?"
For humanistic knowledge, we should ask, "What is its relationship to me?"

# How Liberal Arts Education Changes Lives

Many young people may be passionate about social science, concerned with policy and big data, hoping to improve our lives through them. But the problem is that what we can measure is limited to what we know exists — and even understanding what is already known is difficult.

Art resolves this limitation: by describing human ways of thinking and behavioral habits, we come to understand the potential flaws in our plans for the future.

On the other hand, the humanities are also a history lesson. The past is the source of our thoughts and feelings. At the social level, only by understanding the past can we understand today's laws, social structures, and communal relationships. At the personal level, only by understanding our own past can we grasp the motives behind our words.
