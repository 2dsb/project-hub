The government issued "US dollars" and set an exchange ratio between dollars and gold.

Result: an artificial rule was created — a valueless item was legally declared exchangeable for valuable "gold."

At this point, individual or corporate assets consisted of: physical goods, gold, and US dollars.

Initially, the government issued a certain amount of currency and set an exchange ratio such that the currency in circulation could exactly redeem all the gold in the bank.

1. The government gradually increased the money supply, such that the gold in the bank became insufficient.
2. The government adjusted the dollar-to-gold exchange ratio, reducing the amount of gold each dollar could redeem.
3. The real value of 1 dollar decreased.
4. Prices rose, and consumer confidence declined.
5. Consumer savings decreased.
