## How People Make Decisions

1. **People face trade-offs**
   Individuals allocate time; guns and butter; efficiency versus equality.

2. **The cost of something is what you give up to get it**
   The decision to attend university: the main benefit is enriched knowledge and better job opportunities. The cost is not limited to tuition, books, housing, and other expenses. Two considerations: on one hand, even without attending university, you would still need a place to live; on the other hand, the opportunity cost of time must also be accounted for.

3. **Rational people think at the margin**
   **Marginal change**: a small incremental adjustment to a plan of action. An airline ticket priced at $500 may drop to $300 just before departure, because the marginal benefit of selling one more seat is $300, while the marginal cost is merely a packet of peanuts and a can of soda for the passenger.

4. **People respond to incentives**
   An **incentive** is something (such as the prospect of punishment or reward) that induces a person to act. For example, when the price of apples rises, people decide to eat fewer apples. Incentives alter behavior by changing benefits and costs. Seat belts reduce the probability of injury or death, thereby lowering the cost of driving faster — this is an incentive that leads people to drive faster and less carefully, increasing the accident rate. Pedestrians, lacking such protection, face a higher fatality rate as a result.

## How People Interact

5. **Trade can make everyone better off**
   Trade allows people to specialize in what they do best, and by trading with others, people can obtain a wide variety of goods and services at lower cost.

6. **Markets are usually a good way to organize economic activity**
   In a market economy, the decisions of a central planner are replaced by the decisions of millions of firms and households. Firms decide whom to hire and what to produce; households decide which firm to work for and what to buy with their income.

7. **Governments can sometimes improve market outcomes**
   The invisible hand can only work its magic when the government enforces rules and maintains the institutions that are critical to a market economy.
   - **Property rights** refer to the ability of an individual to own and control scarce resources. A farmer will not plant crops if he expects his harvest to be stolen.
   There are two broad reasons for government intervention that changes how people allocate resources: promoting efficiency or promoting equality.
   - **Promoting efficiency**: market failure (situations where the market fails to allocate resources efficiently on its own), caused by:
       1. **Externalities**: the impact of one person's actions on the well-being of a bystander (e.g., pollution, which the market does not account for on its own)
       2. **Market power**: the ability of a single economic actor to have a substantial influence on market prices
   - **Promoting equality**

## How the Economy as a Whole Works

8. **A country's standard of living depends on its ability to produce goods and services**
   **Productivity** (the quantity of goods and services produced per unit of labor input) drives differences in living standards. The more goods and services a country's workers produce per unit of labor input, the higher the standard of living for its citizens.

9. **Prices rise when the government prints too much money**
   **Inflation**: an increase in the overall level of prices in the economy.
   When the government issues too much money, the value of money falls, the value of goods remains unchanged, and prices rise.

10. **Society faces a short-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment**
    In the **long run**, rising prices are caused by increases in the money supply.
    In the **short run**, an increase in the money supply stimulates the overall level of spending in the economy, thereby increasing the demand for goods and services. Over time, rising demand causes firms to raise prices, while also encouraging them to hire more workers and produce more goods and services. Hiring more workers means lower unemployment.
    Printing money in the short run drives up prices and boosts employment; in the long run, prices adjust. This insight helps analyze the **business cycle** (fluctuations in economic activity such as employment and production).
