Technology in the singular sense causes changes in prices and production relations within the economy.
**Industry adopts technology.**

Technology in the plural sense causes expansive adjustments to economic patterns.
**"Technology bodies encounter industries"** is the better formulation.

# Technology Bodies Encounter Industries
Technology body: the totality of methods, equipment, practices, and understandings that form a technology.
Industry: the combination of organizational structures and commercial processes, production methods and physical equipment.

The application of a technology body is integrated into the elements and processes of an industry; if the resulting combination is particularly powerful, it gives rise to derivative industries or secondary industries.

For example, when options (derivatives) in banking encountered computing technology, futures exchanges and derivatives markets were formed.
This is also called a re-domaining of banking and the computer domain.

## Economic Re-domaining
From the industry's perspective, it refers to existing industries adapting to a technology body:
- Adopting the new technology body
- Extracting from the new technology body, selecting what they need
- Combining parts of the industry's components with components from the new domain
- Creating secondary industries
- Producing a new version of the economy

From the domain's perspective, it refers to the domain responding to the industry:
- Adding new capabilities to meet industry needs

Such **co-variation and co-creation** is called disruptive change (different industries and technology bodies experience overlapping, interacting disruptive changes that collectively reshape the economy).

## Time in the Economy
From the launch of a new domain's core technology to the domain's full impact, it typically takes decades. This itself serves as evidence that the relationship between industry and domain is not one of "the former adopting the latter," but rather a mutual encounter — a re-domaining, a disruptive change. Adoption might involve a 5-10 year delay due to human factors, but never several decades.

From the adolescence to maturity of a technology body:
- The new domain must accumulate adherents and reputation
- It must find purpose and use
- Core technologies must resolve obstacles and fill gaps between components
- Supporting technologies must be developed
- The phenomenological basis and the theories built upon it must be understood
- Markets must be discovered
- Existing economic structures must be restructured to leverage the new domain
- Old configurations must become familiar with the new domain and its intrinsic practices
- This requires finance, institutions, management, government policy, and people skilled in applying the new domain, all working in concert

Thus, the time this process takes is not about the lag between noticing and adopting, but about the time needed to restructure existing economic arrangements and adapt to the domain — which can take decades.

Relational time: a way of describing time through changes in new structures (without change, there is no way to mark time).
From the perspective of relational time, economic re-domaining involves changes in relational time, which occur on two scales:
1. Fast-time: the time it takes for new technologies to be designed, tested, and absorbed by the economy, revealing the pace of change in things, business activities, and new ways of doing things
2. Slow-time: the new technology body entering the economy, manifesting in the economy and society as an era

## Geographic Characteristics
Scientific knowledge flows worldwide, but the frontier of technology body development is usually highly concentrated in one or a few countries. Why?

In reality, truly cutting-edge knowledge does not originate from knowledge itself. Rather, it originates from "esoteric craftsmanship" — a cognitive system:
- Knowing what can work and what cannot
- Knowing which principles and methods are more likely to succeed
- Knowing what parameter values to use in a given technology
- Knowing who to talk to in order to push things through
- Knowing how to salvage overlooked problems
- Knowing what to ignore and what to pay attention to

This craft-based cognition takes science and pure knowledge as given, emerging from a shared, ineffable culture born of common experience.
This also means knowing how to manipulate newly discovered, poorly understood phenomena — and this cognition becomes part of the shared culture.

In these places, trade secrets are also critical — they form the basis for further development of industries and new technologies.

When a country or region leads an industry through **trade secrets and shared cognition**, success follows, attracting more companies and creating a positive feedback loop.

Of course, regional advantages cannot last forever, but the deep understanding of one technology in an advantaged region can be leveraged into deep understanding of another technology.
