> There are three resources related to learning: attention, working memory, and willpower. All of them are finite.

# Attention: One at a Time
Attention refers to how many things a person can consciously focus on at once. Humans cannot multitask — in fact, only one task can be handled at any given moment.

But we must be wary of "seemingly single-tasking, actually multitasking." For example, writing: when you are choosing words and phrasing on one hand while editing and proofreading on the other, the former requires diffuse attention and the latter requires focused attention — that is also a form of "multitasking."

# Working Memory
Unlike attention, working memory refers to things you are not necessarily focused on, but which "pop into your mind" while you are attending to something else. Working memory holds about 7 to 9 items, though the exact number is not what matters.

This is where the Zeigarnik effect comes into play. It refers to the phenomenon where **an unfinished task occupies a person's short-term memory**, potentially interfering with what they are actually trying to do.

The way to prevent this is to write unfinished tasks on a to-do list, tricking the brain. Of course, for important problems, you can also deliberately let them occupy working memory, hoping they will spark inspiration at some point.

# Willpower
Willpower refers to the energy you expend when making yourself do something psychologically resistant (consuming), such as making decisions or exercising self-control. It is also finite.

So we need to "increase supply and reduce consumption":
1. Standardize your environment: reduce the number of decisions you need to make, conserving willpower.
2. Rest after completing tasks: recharge your energy and willpower promptly.
