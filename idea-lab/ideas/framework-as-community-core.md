# Framework-as-Core: Building a Community Around the Social-Resources Decomposition

## The Idea

Take the social-resources decomposition — the five-subsystem model of **connections, channels, platforms, endorsements, and information** (from `teach-social-resources`) — and use it as the *core* around which to build and gradually expand a personal community.

The framework becomes the glue: something you can offer, everyone can share, and that keeps a network coherent without constant maintenance.

## The Method

Offline, after a conversation that has genuinely gone well:

1. **Offer the mindset.** Say you have a way of thinking about people and relationships that might be useful to them.

2. **Show the decomposition.** Walk them through the social-resources map at a shallow level — just enough to be useful (lessons 0001–0004), not a full course.

3. **Name the hope.** Express the wish to keep a certain tie strength with them *long-term*, without either of you having to actively maintain it.

4. **Invite them into a group chat** whose operating principle is:
   - every member makes their **position**, **channel**, and **platform** transparent to the others;
   - whenever two members don't yet know each other, a *tie strength that can carry information* is created between them.

Repeat this with new people, expanding the circle **from lower to higher status and ability**, in an order that guarantees every member has something to gain from joining.

## Why It Works

- **The framework is the value you bring.** It's a genuine gift — a model of how social resources work — which gives people a reason to join beyond vague networking.
- **Transparency compounds.** If everyone's position/channel/platform is visible, the group becomes a standing map of who can reach what, so value flows to whoever needs it without you brokering every single exchange.
- **You benefit as the third party.** The idea comes from the *middleman as third-party beneficiary*: when two members who didn't know each other start exchanging information through your group, you gain from being the node that made the connection — the broker's structural benefit.
- **Low maintenance by design — but only via common knowledge.** The "no active maintenance" promise works, but for a subtler reason than "just don't bother." See the correction below.

## Why "No Maintenance" Actually Works — The Common-Knowledge Correction

Left to itself, a tie decays: the fact that *tie strength decays without ongoing contact* (Lesson 0002) is real, and when people don't know it, they simply let the decay happen. Each person privately assumes the tie will hold, neither acts to sustain it, and it silently erodes.

The fix is to make the decay fact **common knowledge**. The framework already contains it, so sharing the decomposition hands every member the same fact. Once two people both know that (a) ties decay without contact and (b) keeping the tie alive is mutually beneficial — *and each knows the other knows this* — the tie can be held without active maintenance. The shared awareness turns "we both let it slide" into "we both keep it alive," and neither party has to deliberately work at it. The group's information flow then does the sustaining automatically, because participating in it is the natural, low-cost consequence of everyone knowing the tie is worth keeping.
