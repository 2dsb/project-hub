# The Fidelity Problem in Knowledge Transfer

## Trigger
Listening to an intern describe how the company works → couldn't build understanding. Reverse thinking: if I tried to describe school to someone who never went, could I make it clear?

## Core Judgment
Most likely not. Because description is an **incremental patch**, not building a new house. The listener has no anchoring experience (the implicit feel of classes, rankings, homeroom teachers), so the patch won't apply.

School's advantage over a company: school is closed, highly repetitive, rules are transparent → at least the explicit rule layer can be explained clearly. A company has massive implicit rules, and even interns themselves are still figuring them out.

## Corollary
Language as a knowledge transfer medium has a fidelity ceiling — it can only patch on top of a shared experiential base; it cannot build experiential understanding from scratch.
