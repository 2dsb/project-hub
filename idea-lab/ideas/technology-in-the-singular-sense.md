# Definition
## Functional Perspective
Technology in the singular sense refers to a means of accomplishing a purpose.

## Manifestations
Technology can be understood both as (at the physical level) devices and as (at the behavioral level) processes. These two ways of understanding are equivalent:
- A device necessarily has a corresponding purpose, and the process of achieving that purpose is its process.
- A technological process necessarily has its physical carrier — the device.

# The "Anatomical" Structural Breakdown of Technology
## Organized Around a Central Principle
At the physical level, technology is composed of parts and components.
At the process level, a technology contains a central concept or principle.

For this central concept or principle to exist in physical form, there must be a series of primary assemblies that execute the core principle, and for these primary assemblies to function smoothly, secondary assemblies are needed for support.

# Hierarchical Structure and Recursion
Setting aside whether something is supporting or core, if we focus only on hierarchy, we can decompose technology into primary integrations, secondary integrations, tertiary integrations, and so on.

- **Hierarchical structure**: the whole technology is the trunk, primary integrations are branches, secondary integrations are twigs.
- **Recursion**: the structure contains some degree of self-similar components — technology is built out of technologies at different levels.

The relationship between higher- and lower-level technologies:
- Higher-level technologies issue commands to lower-level technologies, serving as their purpose.
- Lower-level technologies determine what higher-level technologies can accomplish.

Every technology is potentially ready to become a component of a higher-level technology.

This implies that a change in one technology must be accepted by the higher-level technology (for example, changes in aircraft characteristics require changes in squadron command methods).

## Modularity
**Modularity** is a synonym for integration. Given the definition of technology, modularity can also be understood as the grouping of functions.

Advantages of modularity:
- Better protection against unpredictable changes (if an accident occurs, only a small part, not the entire outcome, is lost) (from a manufacturing perspective).
- Grouping by function simplifies the design process, allowing designers to memorize and attend to each part separately.

Application context for modularity: a functional module is worth the cost of partitioning technology into functional units only when it is reused frequently enough.

This is also how modularity emerges: initially, several parts are linked in series to achieve a purpose. Later, people realize that this purpose is in high enough demand that this serial arrangement of parts is used frequently, and so they modularize it.

## Fluidity
When purpose changes, the integration patterns of technology change, giving rise to new technologies. The flow of technology never stops.
