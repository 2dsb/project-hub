# Two Concepts in Transformation

## Leadership
In the past, leadership meant "putting others' interests before your own," "fighting for ideals," and "contributing to the health of the entire system."
Today, leadership has narrowed to "attaining a high position on the social pyramid," such as becoming the CEO of a Fortune 500 company.

## Serving Others
In the past, serving others stemmed from a sense of social responsibility, and those who did it were humble.
Today, serving others creates an opposition between server and served — people serve with a "charity" mindset. Moreover, their service is actually self-serving: it merely polishes their resume, signals moral superiority, but helps no one.

## The Connection Between the Two
In the past, leadership and serving others were tightly intertwined.
Today, they have become alienated.

# The Reality of Leadership Today
In any company, there is a certain kind of person — perhaps your boss — who has no initiative, no creativity, and no ideas about the environment or the system. The only thing they know is to preserve the status quo. We call such people "caretakers."
The reason caretakers, rather than true experts, occupy high positions is that in today's bureaucratic system, upward mobility is proportional to your ability to navigate within the system. Therefore, building wide connections often means echoing what everyone else says — becoming a person without independent thought.

# Some Phenomena and Their Underlying Causes

## Phenomena
People only solve isolated problems — like food production — or engage in DIY-style social entrepreneurship, such as starting a farm. But no one considers how to change the social structure. More specifically, no one considers engaging in political or economic affairs and attempting to make a more effective impact on the world. (After all, in a society where politics and economics are controlled by the powerful, no matter how good other fields become, it does little to help the public.)

## Root Cause Analysis
Today's elite university students thrive within the current social system, which leaves them devoid of "critical dissatisfaction with the status quo" and unaware of alternatives beyond it. (See Note 7?)

# The Solution (or Rather, What Kind of Person We Should Become)
We should become "good citizens and **thinkers**."
As discussed in the previous note, thinking means recognizing common sense, questioning common sense, and re-examining common sense from new angles.
A thinker, in action, does the following:
- Examines their organization or social environment through a critical lens
- Not only answers questions, but also raises them
- Not only completes tasks, but also reflects on their value
- Charts strategic direction for companies, industries, and even nations

The qualities a thinker needs: strong willpower, the courage to oppose traditional ideas and those who propagate them (family, friends), and the ability to think independently without following the crowd.
