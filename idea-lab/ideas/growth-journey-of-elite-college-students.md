# Elite Family Parents
## "Perfect Expectations" for Their Children
- They hover over their children like helicopters, supervising and guiding them through every task. They believe that a child's development requires a rigorous, supervised, and orderly process to build competence. "Let us handle it" is their mantra.
- Mindset: overprotection. If you do everything right, the future will involve less hardship, giving the child a stable and secure life.
- The pursuit of perfectionism for their children: only the perfect, most outstanding child deserves to be loved, which makes elite schools a necessity.

## Demands on Schools
- Granted, they may believe schools should cultivate creative, independent thinkers — but only on the condition of getting into an elite school first, demanding that everything give way to college admissions and resume-building.

# The Role of High Schools
- Some high school teachers recognize the flaws in the education system and try to introduce different values into their schools, such as a love of learning for its own sake, but the room to maneuver is limited.
- Schools are increasingly adopting a "customer-first" model, submitting to the demands of parents. As a result, despite having excellent teachers and bright students, students receive no real education.

# Children of Elite Families — The Origins of Elite College Students
## Infantilization
- Even after entering college, students do not resist being treated like children.
- This phenomenon stems from "helicopter" parents' supervision of every single thing their children do.

## Struggling Between Validation and Worth, Lacking Freedom
- On one hand, they are born into elite families. If their parents are also alumni of elite schools, the parents will expect them to become elite-school graduates as well, to uphold the family brand; there is also peer competition. They must attend college according to their parents' wishes, or the parents may cut off financial support — so they set off on the path of continuing their parents' mission.
- On the other hand, they are keenly aware that their parents demand they be "the best," and they know that if they fail to get into an elite school, their standing in their parents' eyes will drop.
- When attending an elite school means reluctantly catering to their parents' expectations, they also lose any anticipation for school itself.
- Precisely because of their parents' towering expectations, they are more prone to depression and psychological disorders.

## Losing the Capacity to Feel and to Care
- Elite families do not tolerate their children's frustration or personal needs. Children raised in such families, in order to meet family expectations, never develop their own value system.
- This also leads them to arrive at college lacking any understanding of life's meaning.

## Confidence Built on Success
- They feel confident when they succeed, but the moment they encounter a setback, that confidence collapses. So they study with all their might — every A they earn brings a measure of comfort to cope with the fear of getting a B or failing.
- The excessive pursuit of being the best and being perfect: every time they satisfy their parents' demands, an even higher demand follows, so the sense of fulfillment is always fleeting. But this behavior becomes internalized — the child perpetually feels it is never enough, and must keep chasing perfection. They oscillate between arrogance and self-loathing: arrogant when they achieve perfection, and feeling worthless when they encounter a setback. "Either you're a genius, or you're garbage."
- Seeing others' achievements as a threat: it shatters their self-image of being "the best" and triggers jealousy.

## A Dismal Secular Mindset
- Blind ambition, a habitual aversion to risk, and a Hobbesian, ruthlessly competitive materialism.

# The Relationship Between Parents and Children
The emotional bond between the two is extremely weak. Praise cannot substitute for warmth, and criticism and supervision cannot substitute for attention and guidance. Parents are everywhere — except in their children's hearts.
