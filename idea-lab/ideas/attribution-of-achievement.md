# Attribution of Achievement

## The Idea

A conversational technique for getting to know someone who is accomplished — or who looks likely to become so — and extracting their insight. It works best in person, not online.

You open by expressing genuine admiration for what they've done, then keep pressing a single question — *"what do you attribute your achievement to?"* — and through repeated follow-ups, map out the small set of factors (neither too many nor too few) that, in their mind, their achievement — or their likely future achievement — depends on entirely. Or at least, the factors they can name.

## The Method

1. **Open with admiration.** Lead with respect for their achievement (or their trajectory). This lowers their guard and makes the question feel like homage rather than an interrogation.

2. **Ask for the attribution.** "What do you attribute your success to?" — or, for someone still early in their rise, "what do you think your future success will come down to?"

3. **Chase each answer in two directions:**

   - **(a) Vertical — decompose a single attribution.** Take any factor they name and ask what *causes* it. Then what causes that. Each round of "why" drills one level deeper, turning a surface answer into a causal chain.

   - **(b) Horizontal — test completeness.** Ask *"are these all of the factors?"* — concretely: *"if you had only these factors, would you be just the right amount of luck away from where you are (or where you're heading)?"* If they say yes, the list is complete up to luck. If they say no, they'll name the missing factor — and you keep going.

The goal of the back-and-forth is a **minimal, complete causal model**: exactly the factors the person believes fully determine their achievement, no more and no less.

## Why It Works

- **Admiration-first lowers the barrier.** People answer more readily when they feel respected, not audited.
- **"Attribution" is an open, flattering question.** It invites them to tell their own story of success, which people are eager to do.
- **The two operations force completeness and depth.** (a) stops them at a shallow answer; (b) stops them at a partial list. Together they converge on the full causal picture.
- **The luck framing is disarming.** Asking "are you only luck away?" concedes a factor they'd otherwise be modest about, which makes it easier for them to say what's *not* luck.
