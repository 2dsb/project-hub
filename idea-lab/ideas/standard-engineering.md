Standard engineering, also known as technological innovation (in the singular sense) or "design," can be understood as the process of creating a new technology.

The strict definition is: **the process of creating a new version (itself a technology) of an old technology within a technology domain, using already-familiar combination methods and structures.**

Difficulty spectrum:
From simple to difficult:
- Conventional projects applying standard components and conventions
- Projects requiring practice or experimentation
- Projects with hard-to-break boundaries and special challenges

# The Standard Engineering Process

The general process is:
At the conceptual level, guided by requirements and drawing on familiar combination structures and methods, design the overall conceptual form, then design the core integrated components, then the secondary integrated components, and so on.
At the physical level, realize the above conceptual design materially using various technologies (devices and processes).

This may be hard to grasp, so here is an example:

## Easy Version

Requirement: Design an airplane that can seat 350 people.
Precondition: Technical professionals already have the capability to design an airplane seating 250 people.

The requirement now serves as the purpose of the technology to be developed. Experts will naturally conceive of solutions within the "aircraft manufacturing" domain.

"Already-familiar methods or structures" here refers to the basic structure of an airplane — the experience and practice that an airplane is composed of the main fuselage, engines, flight control systems, landing gear, and other structures combined according to certain rules.

Drawing on these "already-familiar methods or structures," the requirement (the problem to be solved) is broken down into sub-requirements for different components. For instance, 350 people require 9–10 people seated side by side, which demands an increase in fuselage width; further, at the same level, increased fuselage width demands changes to the wings; going further down the hierarchy, increased fuselage width demands changes to the materials used.

## Hard Version

The above process is the easy version because, once the conceptual design is complete, you can build it physically based on the mental design without encountering new problems.

The difficult scenario involves:
- Several core concepts whose feasibility is uncertain
- Components with no off-the-shelf solutions, requiring custom design
- The conceptual design cannot "pass in one go" — it requires testing and balancing
- Human decision-making and coordination are needed, which further triggers inter-team communication issues and stakeholder-related problems

In summary, this boils down to two points:
1. Entering unknown territory and encountering unpredictable problems
2. The human factor

# "Problem-Solving"

Let us first consider: why are engineers always solving problems?
As mentioned above, unpredictable risks and the human factor are two influencing factors, but are they the main ones?

Not really.
This is tied to the nature of demand for technology creation: a new technology only needs to be developed when something about an existing technology needs to be different.
For example: adapting to different physical environments, improving efficiency, swapping in better parts and materials, changes in market demand, etc.

Thus, the purpose of the technology becomes: solving the problem raised by the "demand side."
To solve this problem, first design the overall conceptual form, then design the core integrated components... At each step, new situations arise and new problems emerge; not only must each level's modules be reconsidered, but they must also work together harmoniously.

**From this perspective, design is an attempted solution to a large problem (and all the smaller problems it spawns), and a complete design is a specific solution to a specific engineering problem.**

# Design as Expression

What constitutes a solution to a problem? Or rather, how is a solution constructed?

The engineer first generates an intention, then selects a toolkit or language, then envisions concepts and functions, and selects appropriate components.

This can be compared to the relationship between language and thought: ideas arise from the unconscious, and we use combinations of words to express them.

# The Relationship Between Standard Engineering and Innovation

For standard engineering, the solutions are theoretically numerous; in practice, the same engineer tends to adopt the same approach (from a linguistic perspective, similar phrases and sentence constructions), but projects involving multiple designers produce novel solutions. These solutions and practical experiences are absorbed into the domain, and over time, they form technological innovation.

# The "Darwinism" of Technology

1. Technologies are combined to form new versions.
2. If a technology is used enough times (becomes popular), it forms a standard module (becomes locked in) and becomes an element for constructing new technologies.

## Commonality: Survival of the Fittest

Good technologies are reused and become standardized (this is a positive feedback loop: the more a technology is reused, the more well-known it becomes, the easier it is to be reused again, thus forming a standard).
Bad technologies go unnoticed after solving a specific problem.

## Differences

- Biological evolution has a random direction and takes place over long time intervals.
- Technological evolution has a "direction" driven by demand and is artificially assembled by humans.
