# Domains: Clusters of Technology

We can group technologies by similarity:

- Some technologies exploit phenomena from the same family of phenomena
- Some technologies share the same scientific theory
- Some technologies share a common purpose

These related technologies are grouped together, and the group is called a **domain**.

## Defining and Understanding Domains

A domain is a collection of technologies that either **share a common external form** or **share the latent potential to work toward and serve a common purpose**.

A domain contains multiple technologies and can be understood as a toolbox — a set of interrelated technologies.

## Domain Hierarchy

A domain may contain sub-domains, sub-sub-domains, and so on, much like:

- A large toolbox can contain smaller toolboxes
- A set can have subsets

## Domain Grammar

A domain can also be viewed as a language. Its basic elements are the technologies within the domain, and its **grammar can be understood as the rules for combining technologies** — determining how and under what conditions its elements are assembled.

From this perspective, developing a new technology is expressing something using one language (if drawing technologies from a single domain) or multiple languages (if drawing from different domains).

## Strengths and Limitations of Domains

A domain's strength lies in what it accomplishes most easily — what it is good at:

- If something is converted into a digital form, it can be transformed within the domain of digital technology. In the physical world, we more readily flatten or move an object physically.

A domain also has limitations:

- For example, the digital domain provides clarity, but it cannot evaluate whether something is fashionable.

# Domain Definition: The Origin of Technology Design

Engineering design for a technology begins with choosing a domain — equivalent to selecting a set of technologies with the potential to achieve a goal, or choosing a toolbox.

For example, when an architect designs a building, they may choose a glass-steel combination over concrete. This process is **domain definition**.

## Re-domaining: Innovation in the Plural Sense

**Re-domaining** means: to achieve an established goal, one originally used a certain domain, but now uses a new domain. This change of domain is called re-domaining.

Re-domaining and domain-level innovation are closely related.

Suppose a set of goals was originally achieved using Domain A. Now, built around new phenomena or theories, Domain B emerges and may also achieve this set of goals. Under what conditions would Domain B replace Domain A?

1. Domain B offers new and greater possibilities compared to Domain A — that is, more potential technologies.
   This inspires technical experts, gradually turning Domain B's potential technologies into reality, and over time Domain A is displaced.
   For example: from the domain of mechanical technology to the domain of electronic technology. Electronic technology covers the purposes that mechanical technology can achieve, but with richer applications — so the electronic technology domain flourished.

2. The technologies within Domain B can be combined in diverse ways.
   The technologies here do not have only one or a handful of combination patterns. On the contrary, they offer many ways to combine.
