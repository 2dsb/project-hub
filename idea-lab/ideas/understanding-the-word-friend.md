Common sense (my view): Friends should be permanent, solid

But I've also read in books about many friends who "got along well" eventually falling out over a single remark

Logically speaking, you also can't determine whether a friendship is permanent or whether there's a possibility of falling out in the future

> (My view) Friends should be permanent, solid
>
> vs
>
> (Opposing view) Friends should be seen as temporary fellow travelers

> (My view) Only those you truly open up to are "real friends"?
>
> vs
>
> (Opposing view) As long as you have "conversational chemistry," that already counts as friendship?

This topic can extend to love: for example,

> (My view) From one person's perspective in a relationship, is dating essentially a value exchange? (I provide emotional value to the other person, and they provide emotional value to me in return? Or like the congressman couple in House of Cards, using each other, cooperating out of shared ambition)
>
> vs
>
> (Opposing view) Love reflects human animal nature: involuntary mating behavior driven by instinct and sensation — just do it, no need to ponder its essence

This raises the following questions:

1. How do friendships (and similar interpersonal relationships) form? How do they change? How do they end? (empirical)
2. What ideas underlie my "common sense"?
3. How can this view and its opposing view be unified?
4. Analyze the phenomenon: In daily life, I always think of zyw, but I first frame it as friendship. One possibility is "I like her" — is this a form of defense mechanism?
5. Analyze the phenomenon: In daily life, I chat very little — only when there's something specific to discuss.
6. Analyze my social pattern:
   - In the first 1-2 weeks of knowing someone, I actively and proactively try to discuss serious topics with them
   - Some people lack "depth" in my perception — toward them I become passive afterward, or only chat about entertainment topics, eventually becoming nodding acquaintances
   - Some people have "depth" in my perception — I genuinely respect and appreciate them, then try to make them "permanent friends"
