Science is the formal knowledge of phenomena.

# Science and Phenomena

1. Science provides the knowledge needed to understand phenomena and explains them.
2. Science predicts how phenomena behave.
3. Science provides the means to observe phenomena.
4. Science provides methods to discover and harness phenomena.

# Science and Technology

In [21a The Gene of Technology: Phenomena](../permanent-notes/21a-技术的基因：现象.md), technology harnesses phenomena.

As stated above, science explains phenomena, predicts phenomena, and provides methods for discovering phenomena.

So what is the relationship between science and technology?

## Is Technology the Application of Science?

Not exactly. In the past, before science had emerged, technology already existed. It was not until the mid-19th century that technology became bundled with science. The reason: **ancient phenomena were intuitive and visible to the naked eye, whereas modern phenomena cannot be directly captured — they require science to explain them and provide observation methods before they can be further harnessed and turned into technology.**

## Science Within Technology

Phenomena that can be explained by science are domesticated and formed into technology. Over time, through technological practice, people accumulate experience and develop theories about technology — and science is internalized within this process. **Technology is built from both science and its own accumulated experience.**

## Technology Within Science

**Science is built upon technology.**

- In *point 1 — "Science provides the knowledge needed to understand phenomena and explains them"* — the goal of scientific theory is to explain a phenomenon clearly; this is its purpose. It is composed of conceptual terms logically combined and arranged in a hierarchical structure, similar to how technology is assembled.
- In *point 3 — "Science provides the means to observe phenomena"* — these means include instruments and equipment, which are themselves technology.
- When it comes to scientific experiments, rigorous experimentation is itself a form of technology: its goal is "the exploration of natural principles."

## Science Is Not Technology

On one hand, science and technology share a similar structural form: a method for understanding, inquiring into, and explaining phenomena — and this method itself contains various sub-methods.

But science is more than the sum of its content. It is also:
- A conviction — that nature is fundamentally knowable, capable of being investigated and attributed to causes.
- A set of methods for thinking and practice.
- A culture.
