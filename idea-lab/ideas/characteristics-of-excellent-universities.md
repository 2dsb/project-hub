# Students

More interesting, more curious, more open — like partners rather than competitors.

They are people you can bounce ideas off of, discuss literature and art with, and who, facing the same graduation pressures, can support one another.

# Entry and Exit

## Guiding Students In

A good university does not simply hand a course catalog to young people, hold an orientation event, and then step back to let students figure things out on their own.

Instead, it offers an "Introduction to Liberal Arts" course — a humanities-based syllabus covering the essentials of each discipline, designed to help students broaden their interests and find a direction for future study.

## Transitioning from Undergraduate to Post-University Life

A good school proactively introduces students across all majors to various opportunities and is willing to make long-term investments in you.

Unlike some universities that only start preparing seniors in their final year and repackage "vocational skills" as "leadership," excellent universities run year-round learning programs that offer thorough, systematic preparation for life after graduation. For example, Bennington College's Center for Social Good allows students to choose a theme — poverty, public health, or the environment — and conduct project-based inquiry. The underlying philosophy is to use one's own resources to advance social progress (which is, in essence, what "leadership" actually means).
