The humanities and the arts are, at their core, about cultivating the capacity for thought through the wisdom of past thinkers. Those who live below the execution line are powerless to change their own circumstances, so the responsibility for changing this reality falls on the "leadership class."

This ties into the idea of "service to others." In today's universities, service to others has been twisted into two opposing camps: us-versus-them antagonism on one side, and charitable acts on the other. True liberal arts education, by contrast, fosters critical thinking — understood at the societal level as serving the public good — that analyzes and discards the common sense handed down by the university and replaces it with one's own understanding. Such an education makes people recognize the value of "providing opportunity" for those in poverty, and they come to see that this kind of change can only be pursued through reforming social institutions. At the same time, literary works allow readers to experience how different people live, which helps in understanding the lived reality of the poor. The ultimate outcome is to guide the transformation of social institutions.
