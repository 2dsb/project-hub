# No Language Mixing Pattern

## The Pattern

When practicing English, I used to have a subtle but strict rule: **never mix Chinese and English in the same sentence**. Pure English or pure Chinese — no hybrid.

## Past Self's Explanation

At the time, I wrote something about not wanting to be judged and feeling tense. (I can no longer fully decode my own explanation.)

## Present Self's Questions

- Why was I so tense about mixing? Was it perfectionism, fear of looking incompetent, or something else?
- Was this rule helpful (forced full immersion) or harmful (added unnecessary anxiety)?
- When did this pattern fade, and why?
- Does it relate to the English correction mode I now use — where corrections are expected and welcomed rather than feared?

## Possible Connection

The current English correction mode might be the evolved form of this: instead of *avoiding* judgment by refusing to mix, I now *invite* judgment by asking for corrections. Same underlying concern (accuracy, not wanting to sound wrong), opposite strategy (avoidance → exposure).
