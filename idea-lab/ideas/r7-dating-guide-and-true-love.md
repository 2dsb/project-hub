In r7, women judge whether a man is "powerful" by "feeling" — a candlelit dinner moves them, not 100 bitcoins in a wallet.

But isn't the source of love also rooted in some physiological (gene-driven) feeling? Coincidentally, a candlelit dinner can trigger this physiological feeling — does this mean that in modern society, human genes haven't fully adapted to "social change"?

Maybe I haven't fully articulated this. In this world, some women fall for a man because of the "romantic atmosphere," but everyone knows this "romantic atmosphere" comes from money (yet they feel nothing toward money itself). Isn't this contradictory?

# Clarifying the Question

First, a concrete scenario. Initially B doesn't know G1. After they meet, B invites G1 to a street-side diner. Amid the noisy meal, B confesses his feelings. G1 will not accept.

Same B and G1. Initially B doesn't know G1. After they meet, B invites G1 to a candlelit dinner. In the romantic atmosphere, B confesses his feelings. G1 accepts.

All factors about the man are controlled except the dining location.

Now we want to know: what is the main factor behind this shift?

1. Did the man's feelings change? No change.
2. Between the two settings, is there a difference in the man's charm? Clearly yes (charm here understood from the woman's perspective).
3. Where does the change in the man's charm come from? (Transference?)
     In the romantic atmosphere created by the candlelit dinner:
     - The woman immerses herself in the atmosphere, feels joy and happiness, blurs the boundary between feeling and reason, attributes the emotion to the man across from her, believes she genuinely likes him and is genuinely liked by him.
     - Similar in principle to taking a woman to watch a horror movie.

So we can say: a woman's emotions are easily influenced by environment, and environment can be altered by money. Yet even knowing that B2, who has 100 bitcoins, can casually provide candlelit dinner settings, she still scoffs at B2 who merely shows her 100 bitcoins and confesses.

## Justification for This Thought Experiment

Event A1 is factual. Based on A1, imagine changing one element of A1 to obtain event A2 (imagined, not actual). A2's outcome is easily predictable. Comparing and analyzing A1 against A2 is reasonable.

*she still scoffs at B2 who merely shows her 100 bitcoins and confesses* — "she" indeed refers to G2.

But in this experiment, B is a specific person, while the role of G1 is played by over 10 people, and the role of G2 also by over 10. If G1 and G2 were just one person each, of course it couldn't be proven — but the sample size ensures the conclusion's credibility.

## On "Charisma"

Charisma isn't just two states — "has charisma" and "lacks charisma." What we're concerned with is how so-called charisma changes across environments — for example, a romantic atmosphere increases a man's charisma in a woman's eyes.
