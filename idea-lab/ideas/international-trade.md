# Definition

World price: the price of a good prevailing in world markets.
Tariff: a tax on imported goods.

# International Trade

10 Principles: 5. Trade can make everyone better off.

## Equilibrium Without Trade

Isoland is a country initially isolated from the rest of the world, with no international trade. In this scenario, the country's textile market consists entirely of domestic buyers and sellers, forming an equilibrium quantity and equilibrium price.

## If Free Trade Is Allowed

1. Question 1: Would Isoland become a textile importer or exporter?

If the domestic price is higher than the world price, it becomes an importer; conversely, if the domestic price is lower than the world price, it becomes an exporter.

This reflects whether the country has a comparative advantage in textile production.

2. Question 2: Who are the winners and losers?

Assume Isoland is a small economy that cannot influence the world price (a price taker).
If it is an exporter, when the domestic price rises to equal the world price, domestic supply increases, domestic demand decreases, and the gap is the export volume.

![[IMG_0161.jpeg]]

Textile producers are better off, consumers are worse off, and total surplus increases.

If it is an importer, similarly, producers are worse off, consumers are better off, and total surplus increases.

# Tariffs

Tariffs only affect importing countries. They raise the price of imported textiles above the world price, narrowing the gap with the domestic price. This makes buyers worse off, sellers better off, reduces import volume, and pushes the domestic market toward the no-trade equilibrium.

Tariffs create deadweight loss.

# Other Benefits of International Trade

1. Increased variety of goods
2. Economies of scale lower costs
3. Increased competition
4. Enhanced exchange of ideas

# Arguments for Restricting Trade

1. Job destruction
     Workers can transition from the textile industry to industries where the country has a comparative advantage.
2. The national-security argument
3. The infant-industry argument
4. The unfair-competition argument
     Firms in different countries are subject to different laws and regulations.
5. The protection-as-a-bargaining-chip argument
     Problem: the threat may not be credible.
