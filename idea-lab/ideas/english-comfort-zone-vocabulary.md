# English Comfort Zone Vocabulary

## The Blind Spot

Once I build up a set of "natural expressions" I'm comfortable with, I tend to **keep using them** — the familiar words, the familiar sentence structures. I'm likely avoiding unfamiliar words and alternative phrasings without even noticing I'm doing it.

This is a local optimum: fluency feels good, so I stay where fluency is easy. But the expression bank stops growing.

## Why It's Hard to Detect

Unlike explicit errors (which the correction mode catches), this is an **error of omission** — nothing looks wrong, nothing triggers a correction. The problem is what's *not* being said.

## Possible Solutions

| Approach | Mechanism |
|----------|-----------|
| **Vocabulary quota** | Require X new/unfamiliar words per session. AI tracks and flags when quota isn't met. |
| **Expression rotation** | AI maintains a list of "expressions used recently" and suggests alternatives that haven't appeared in N sessions. |
| **Deliberate stretch** | Before each session, pick 3-5 challenge expressions that must appear at least once in the conversation. |
| **Diversity metric** | AI periodically measures vocabulary diversity (type-token ratio, hapax count) and alerts when it plateaus. |
| **Forced rephrase** | After a session, AI picks 3 sentences I said and asks: "Say this again, but without using word X." |

## Connection to Implicit Improvement

Implicit improvement (tetr.io, English fluency) happens through repetition without conscious access to the mechanism. But the comfort zone problem is the **shadow side** of the same process: implicit learning can converge to a local optimum and stay there indefinitely. You need a deliberate perturbation to escape.
