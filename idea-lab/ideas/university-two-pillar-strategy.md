# University Four-Year Strategy: Two Pillars, Run in Parallel

## Source

A conversation with a senior entrepreneur (started his company as a junior, now runs a 100-person team) at a CKGSB (长江商学院) event. The conversation crystallized a complete university strategy.

## Context

Author is an 18-year-old incoming freshman at Peking University, School of Mathematical Sciences (北大数院).

## Critical: Both Pillars Run Simultaneously

These are not sequential phases. From day one of university, skill-building and network-building advance **at the same time**. Each year, energy is split across both tracks. The social positions don't wait for skill mastery — they **enable** it by surfacing opportunities (internships, research openings, mentor introductions) that feed back into the skill track.

```
Year 1  ████████████████████  Skill: Map industry, choose sub-field, begin core courses
        ████████████████████  Social: Class monitor + enter External Relations dept

Year 2  ████████████████████  Skill: Deepen coursework, start research, first competitions
        ████████████████████  Social: Rise within External Relations, build industry contacts

Year 3  ████████████████████  Skill: Research output, advanced courses, target internships
        ████████████████████  Social: Math Dept Student Union President

Year 4  ████████████████████  Skill: Capstone projects, publication, job/startup preparation
        ████████████████████  Social: Leverage full network for next step (funding or job)
```

## Pillar 1: Skill — Industry-First Reverse Planning

### Domain Bet

One of two directions: **Artificial Intelligence** or **Embodied Intelligence (具身智能)**. The chosen sub-field within one of these becomes the four-year focus.

### Two Career Paths

Both assume four years of deep specialization in a sub-field to a "production-grade" level (top 0.1%):

1. **Direct startup path**: If a product idea emerges from deep expertise during university → raise funding after graduation → start a company immediately
2. **Industry-first path**: Graduate → work at a major tech company for 3-5 years → then start a company

The core difference is timing of entrepreneurship, not whether to do it.

### The Reverse Planning Method (Industry → University)

Instead of starting from university courses or personal interests, work backward from industry reality:

1. **Company & product mapping**: Survey companies and products in AI / embodied intelligence. Products matter more than companies — they reveal what the market actually demands.
2. **Industry chain decomposition**: From products, map out the full industry chain — upstream, midstream, downstream, and the relationships between them.
3. **Sub-field identification**: A "sub-field" = one component of the industry chain. Choose based on potential interest.
4. **Course back-tracing**: From the chosen sub-field, reverse-engineer: *Which courses do I need to become an expert in this sub-field?*

### The All-In Principle

Everything in university funnels into this one direction — courses, research, internships, competitions. No scattered energy. Four years, one direction, production-grade depth.

## Pillar 2: Social — The Leadership Ladder

A deliberate, stepwise ascent through university leadership positions, designed to maximize social capital, information flow, and opportunity access:

### Phase 1: Class Monitor (Year 1)
- Entry point into university social structure
- Direct access to classmates, faculty, and administrative staff
- Builds reputation as reliable and organized

### Phase 2: Student Union — External Relations Department (Year 1-2)
- An existing connection (a senior student) already inside the department
- **External relations is the strategic choice**: it interfaces with companies, sponsors, and off-campus organizations — this is the department that builds **industry-facing** connections
- Sponsorship negotiations → practice in business communication
- Corporate partnerships → direct exposure to companies in AI/embodied intelligence
- Alumni engagement → access to mentors who have walked the path

### Phase 3: Math Department Student Union President (Year 3)
- The culmination of the ladder
- Controls agenda, resource allocation, and access within the department
- Maximum visibility — companies, alumni, and faculty all route through the president
- Maximum information flow and opportunity capture

## Why Two Pillars

Neither pillar alone is sufficient:

| Pillar | Focus | Method |
|--------|-------|--------|
| **Skill (硬实力)** | Sub-field expertise | Industry-first reverse planning, all-in course/research/internship selection |
| **Social (软实力)** | Network & opportunities | Class monitor → external relations → student union president ladder |

Deep expertise without a network → no one knows you exist. A network without deep expertise → nothing to offer when opportunities arise. Both must be built in parallel across all four years.

## Why This Works

- The reverse deduction ensures the chosen field has commercial viability from day one — no "solution looking for a problem" at graduation
- Deep specialization in a narrow sub-field creates genuine scarcity (top 0.1%)
- The two-path structure removes the false pressure of "must start a company right after graduation" while keeping entrepreneurship as the terminal goal
- The social ladder is not "joining a club" — it's building a parallel networking engine that feeds directly into the career path
