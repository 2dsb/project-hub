# MOOC's Impact on Universities

Universities use MOOCs to reinforce the hierarchy within the higher education system, further expanding the prestige of top-tier institutions.

Harvard students can interact with professors, while San Jose State University students can only watch them interact — this elevates the former and diminishes the latter.

Universities are most averse to brand dilution. When online platform courses offer credits, universities may treat the matter more seriously, but they typically insist on students living on campus, providing opportunities to interact with experts, cutting-edge facilities, and rich extracurricular activities.

# Universities' Stance on MOOCs

The primary target of MOOCs and for-profit platforms like Coursera is not the students taking online courses, but rather selling online courses to second- and third-tier universities as a revenue stream.

# Questionable Real-World Impact

On one hand, exercises lose their rigor, and students receive no detailed feedback.

On the other hand, video replaces reading, and professors become hosts of celebrity channels — this fuels the rise of the professor-as-celebrity phenomenon.

Despite massive investment, the impact of MOOCs has yet to materialize. Only 4% of users complete a course. The vast majority are adults who already know what they want — they seek targeted upskilling opportunities. Yet "self-orientation" is precisely a capability that university was supposed to cultivate.
