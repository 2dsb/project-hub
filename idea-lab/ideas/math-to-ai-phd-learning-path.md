# The Math-to-AI-PhD Learning Path

Distilled from a chat with a mentor — a PKU math senior who applied to 10/10 PhD programs and works in AI — advising a PKU math freshman (数学英才班) headed toward an AI PhD. Book names that appeared only as images are marked (img) and unnamed; the mentor's recommendations are his own "best-taught" picks and carry a personal-taste caveat.

## Freshman Year — Three Threads

Three foundational threads, learned **together** (not sequentially) and **integrated** (融会贯通):

1. **Analysis**
2. **Algebra**
3. **Probability & Statistics**

Within a thread there is only an **elementary → advanced** (初级→进阶) split — no cross-thread ordering. **Principle: domestic textbooks are far from enough; read foreign textbooks.**

### Analysis (数学分析)
- The standard course text (likely your assigned one) has a weaker viewpoint.
- **Recommended: 张筑生 (Zhang Zhusheng)** — more comprehensive; dissects from bottom-level logic instead of dumping concepts up front.
- **Optional challenge:** two extra books (img) to test your fundamentals.

### Algebra
- **Elementary:** two linear-algebra books (img).
- **Advanced:** one book (img).
- **抽象代数 (abstract algebra)** comes later, outside the elementary tier.

### Probability & Statistics
- **Elementary:** one recommended book (img).
- **Later (概率论/数理统计):** defer to PKU's own recommendations — the field shifts fast with AI-algorithm development. No single must-read; after the basics, read as much as possible.

## Sophomore Year — Three Gates

In order (each a "gate"):

1. **常微分方程 (ODEs)** — first gate.
2. **实分析 (Real Analysis)** — second gate.
3. **泛函分析 (Functional Analysis)** — the hardest gate.

Past these: **偏微分 (PDEs), 测度论 (measure theory), 复分析 (complex analysis), 随机过程 (stochastic processes), 时间序列 (time series)**, etc.

**"前面的融会贯通，后面的一通百通"** — integrate the earlier material thoroughly and everything after comes easily.

## Which Subjects Matter for AI

- **Useful:** 实分析 (Real Analysis).
- **Useless for AI but required:** 泛函分析 (Functional Analysis) — required course, no easy/basic problems; study it seriously or fail.
- **Useless, likely electives:** 拓扑 (Topology), 分形 (Fractals) — don't need to take seriously.
- **Useful but trivial:** 解析几何 (Analytic Geometry) — useful, yet so simple it's not worth mentioning.
- **Everything else: study seriously.**

## How Math Learning Actually Works

- **No shortcuts, no AI tutoring for foundations** — handing book PDFs to an agent to "explain" does not reduce the difficulty of understanding. Math is theoretical; 信科 (CS) is practical, which is why AI tutoring suits CS but not math comprehension.
- Math demands you **grasp the self-consistent logic** (悟到那个自洽的逻辑).
- Grades are **bimodal**: near-perfect or barely-passing, almost nothing between. Understand it → the exam has nothing hard, only carelessness costs marks; don't → you can only do entry-level problems.

## AI Self-Study Path

- **西瓜书 (Zhou Zhihua, *Machine Learning*)** and **花书 (*Deep Learning*)** — chew through both several times.
- 花书 stops at ~2016. After that, read **papers**.
- Landmark: **2017 Transformer — "Attention Is All You Need"** (arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762), origin of LLMs; the literature gets messy/scattered after it.
- **Method:** on arXiv, read papers by **OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, DeepSeek** (the practitioner groups). Skip PKU's own AI team — low quality.
- **CS is a tool** — pick it up on the side; not a separate course of study.
- Optional reference: the AI college's course list, but self-study directly.

## The PhD Track (direct PhD, abroad)

- **Target 常青藤 (Ivy League).** Math majors are highly valued — STEM, math is the "S".
- **Start preparing in sophomore year; apply directly for PhD.**
- **Correct a misconception:** 硕士 and 博士 are both "研究生" (graduate). Abroad is a direct-PhD system — master's and PhD are **two separate tracks**, not sequential.
- **Pick an AI professor with a frontier research direction; from sophomore year, learn their direction and topics.** A PhD = applying to research alongside a professor in the same field.
- **Full scholarship is standard** — tuition-free + monthly stipend.
- **Build strong relationships with the core professors in your major.**

## English and Relationships

- **Start English early** — the mentor's binding constraint: applied 10/10, but his TOEFL score was too low to clear the Ivy League threshold.
- **Keep good relations with classmates** — alumni are your most direct future network. The mentor admits he did this poorly and now spends time making up for it.

## The Mentor's Meta-Lesson

- **Narrow information is expensive.** He entered with very narrow information and "吃了大亏" (suffered a big loss) specifically on the graduate/PhD-track question. The value of this roadmap is that it front-loads information he had to learn the hard way.
