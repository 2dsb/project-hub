# Phenomena and Principles

Every technology depends on phenomena — regularities that exist independently of human discovery, whether natural laws, social patterns, or otherwise, such as gravity.

But if phenomena exist independently of humans, how do we turn them into technology? This means phenomena must be "tamed" — made usable and controllable by humans. We need sub-technologies whose purpose is precisely to harness a given phenomenon.

But what does "controllable" mean in terms of how these phenomena should be combined? This is where principles come in — a principle is a conceptual scheme for how phenomena combine to achieve a purpose.

# To Put It More Clearly

Take a technology A, paired with a principle A. Principle A conceptually explains how technology A harnesses phenomena to achieve its purpose, while technology A is the physical realization of principle A.

The formulation of principle A involves several phenomena. Each phenomenon physically requires a technology to "tame" it so that it conforms to the principle.

For example, principle A involves phenomenon 1, phenomenon 2, and phenomenon 3, along with corresponding technology 1, technology 2, and technology 3, and purpose 1, purpose 2, and purpose 3: technology 1 runs first, then technology 2, then technology 3. If the specified result is achieved, the process ends; otherwise, it loops back to technology 1.

- Technology A (Principle A, Purpose A)
    - Phenomenon 1 ~ Technology 1 (Principle 1, Purpose 1)
        - Phenomenon b ~ Technology b (Principle b, Purpose b)
        - ...
    - Phenomenon 2 ~ Technology 2 (Principle 2, Purpose 2)
    - Phenomenon 3 ~ Technology 3 (Principle 3, Purpose 3)
    - ...
- Principle A: Phenomenon 1 is applied in such-and-such a way, then phenomenon 2 in such-and-such a way, then phenomenon 3... If the purpose is achieved, the process ends; otherwise, loop back to phenomenon 1.
- Purpose 1: Fulfill the function that principle A assigns to phenomenon 1 within technology A.
- Technology 1: The technology that achieves purpose 1.
