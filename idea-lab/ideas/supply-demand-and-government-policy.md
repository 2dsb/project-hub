# Price Controls

Price ceiling: the legal maximum price at which a good can be sold.

Price floor: the legal minimum price at which a good can be sold.

Non-binding: when the price ceiling is above the equilibrium price, it has no effect.

Binding: when the price ceiling is below the equilibrium price — in this case, the price cannot rise to the equilibrium, creating a shortage.

**When the government imposes a binding price ceiling on a competitive market, a shortage of the good results, and sellers must ration the scarce good among a large number of potential buyers.**

Similarly, **a binding price floor causes a surplus of the good** (e.g., minimum wage as a price floor).

# Taxation

- Taxes dampen market activity and reduce the equilibrium quantity.
- Buyers and sellers share the tax burden.
- **Taxing sellers and taxing buyers is equivalent.**

## Elasticity and Tax Incidence

The tax burden falls more heavily on the side of the market that is less elastic.

Payroll taxes: workers bear more of the tax burden.
