Perfectionism is the belief that one must do everything perfectly. Only by achieving perfection does one gain a sense of accomplishment — but that accomplishment is fleeting. A new goal immediately appears, and since perfection has not yet been reached on this new goal, anxiety drives the person to pursue perfection once again, forming a cycle.

A feedback loop refers to a person continuously improving through frequent, small pieces of feedback.

Perfectionism can be seen as a kind of feedback loop — after all, both the sense of accomplishment and anxiety are forms of feedback. The difference is that the feedback in perfectionism does not stem from the characteristics of the task being completed, but from negative or positive emotions. These emotions are unrelated to the specific goal and arise instead from the psychological trait of perfectionism itself. In contrast, feedback in a true feedback loop originates from the task itself. Moreover, due to their respective natures, perfectionist feedback consumes energy, whereas a feedback loop reduces energy expenditure (analogous to the engine in [Where Does Innovation Come From?](where-does-innovation-come-from.md)).
