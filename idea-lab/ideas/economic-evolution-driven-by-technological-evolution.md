# Two Views of the Economy

One view is that the economy is a system of production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services.
Under this view, the economy utilizes technology — when a new technology emerges, part of the system is replaced, a new upgraded module is inserted, and the rest of the machine automatically rebalances; prices, production, and consumption adjust accordingly.

A new way of understanding is that the economy is constructed by technology, consisting of two parts:
- The technological part: the economy is the arrangement of goods and services (as purpose; thus, to some extent, the economy is a purposive system)
- The non-technological part: activities, decision-making, logistics, capital flows, etc.

The technological part is the main body; the non-technological part forms around it.

# How New Technology Causes Structural Change in the Economy

New technology:
1. Enters the economy
2. Replaces old technology, causing the loss of a series of opportunity niches and the resulting chain reactions
3. Provides potential new elements, triggering the emergence of new organizational institutions that incorporate it ([Evolution of Technology in a General Sense](../ideas/evolution-of-technology-in-general-sense.md))
4. Establishes opportunity niches for further technologies — such niches arise from the technological, economic, and social problems caused by the new technology, creating demand for solutions to these problems

Note: technology has not only economic impacts but also secondary impacts on psychology and other domains.

# The Economy as Technology: Its Own Evolution

As also described in [Evolution of Technology in a General Sense](../ideas/evolution-of-technology-in-general-sense.md), new arrangements both replace elements in the economy (in a general sense), causing chain reactions, and create new opportunity niches, generate new demands, and provide new combinatorial possibilities.

# Invariants in Economic Evolution

Human behavioral preferences, the actual structure under discussion, the self-evident truth of supply-demand equilibrium, and other such demands.

**Evolution proceeds in a "problem-and-solution, challenge-and-response" pattern.**
