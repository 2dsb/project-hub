# Markets
A market: a group of buyers and sellers of a particular good or service.
A competitive market: a market with many buyers and sellers, such that each has a negligible impact on the market price.
Perfectly competitive market:
1. The goods offered for sale are identical.
2. There are so many buyers and sellers that no single buyer or seller can influence the market price.
Such buyers and sellers are called price takers.
A monopolist: there is only one seller, and that seller determines the price.
# Demand
Quantity demanded: the amount of a good that buyers are willing and able to purchase.
Law of demand: all else equal, when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls; when the price falls, the quantity demanded rises.
Demand curve: the relationship between quantity demanded and price.
Market demand: the horizontal sum of all individual buyers' demand curves.

## Shifts in the Demand Curve
Market demand assumes other conditions are held constant. If other conditions change, the demand curve shifts.
When quantity demanded increases at every price level, the demand curve shifts to the right — this is called an **increase in demand**. The opposite is called a **decrease in demand**.
### Income
Normal good: all else equal, an increase in income leads to an increase in quantity demanded.
Inferior good: all else equal, an increase in income leads to a decrease in quantity demanded.
### Substitutes
When a rise in the price of one good causes an increase in the demand for another good, the two goods are substitutes (e.g., frozen yogurt and ice cream).
### Complements
When a rise in the price of one good causes a decrease in the demand for another good, the two goods are complements (e.g., gasoline and automobiles).

### Expectations
Your expectations about the future affect your current demand. For example, if you expect the price of ice cream to rise in the future, you will buy more of it now.

# Supply
Quantity supplied: the amount of a good that sellers are willing and able to sell.
Law of supply: all else equal, when the price of a good rises, the quantity supplied increases; when the price falls, the quantity supplied decreases.
Supply curve: the relationship between quantity supplied and price.
Market supply: the horizontal sum of all individual sellers' supply curves.
## Shifts in the Supply Curve
Market supply assumes other conditions are held constant. If other conditions change, the supply curve shifts.
When quantity supplied increases at every price level, the supply curve shifts to the right — this is called an **increase in supply**. The opposite is called a **decrease in supply**.
### Input Prices
When input prices rise, quantity supplied decreases.

### Expectations
If firms expect the price to rise in the future, they will store goods now, reducing current supply.

# Equilibrium
The point where the supply curve and the demand curve intersect is called the equilibrium point.
**Equilibrium price, equilibrium quantity**
Surplus: supply exceeds demand.
Shortage: demand exceeds supply.
Law of supply and demand: the price of any good will adjust spontaneously to bring supply and demand into balance.
## A Method for Analyzing How an Event Affects Equilibrium
1. Determine whether the event shifts the supply curve, the demand curve, or both.
2. Determine the direction of the shift.
3. Use a supply-and-demand diagram to illustrate the change in equilibrium (changes in equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity).
## Key Distinctions
1. Change in supply
2. Change in demand
3. Change in quantity supplied
4. Change in quantity demanded
