# The Sunk-Cost Tug Survives the Right Decision

Context: ~10 hours spent optimizing `/teach` (2026-08-24). Rolling back means that effort "goes to waste."

> **⚠ Beware sunk cost.** It is the standing temptation behind every rollback decision — invested effort arguing for continuing. The correct decision is made on future value alone, never on the hours already spent. Win the action, harvest the lessons, and walk away.

## What Went Right — the Action Level

The rollback happened anyway. Resistance to sunk cost was won at the **action level**: the decision rested on future value (the old default-settings skill works great), not on past effort. Not yielding to the pull is the actual victory — and it is rarer than it sounds, because of the next point.

## What to Watch — the Emotional Level

The decision was made under real duress — genuine agonizing about whether to continue, driven by unwillingness to see 10 hours wasted. Winning the action does not mean escaping the tug; the two are separable. The temptation is a standing force that must be re-fought each time, not a one-time battle that stays won. **Takeaway: beware sunk cost every time — winning once does not disarm it.**

## The Disarming Reframe: Salvage

"Rolling back wastes the 10 hours" is false when insights are extracted. The failed optimization produced the fundamental model, the MAP.md design, the goal-predicate f design, and this post-mortem (see [[plugin-architecture-vs-defaults]]) — all now recorded as ideas. A failed experiment whose lessons are harvested is not wasted; it is expensive tuition. Framing the rollback as *harvesting* rather than *wasting* disarms the sunk-cost argument at its root.
