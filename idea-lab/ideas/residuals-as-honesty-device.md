# Residuals Table as Structural Honesty Device

**Insight**: A residuals section — a table of content that resisted structuring, with explicit reasons — is not a failure of the framework. It's a transparency mechanism. It prevents the silent failure mode where a framework *looks* complete but silently dropped important content.

**Why this works**: Knowing what's NOT in the model is itself structural information. The residuals table transforms incompleteness from a hidden defect into a visible feature. Future-you, returning to the doc after a gap, can see at a glance: "These 4 things are intentionally not here, for these reasons." Without this, future-you has to re-discover the gaps — or worse, assumes completeness and acts on a partial model.

**Contrast with the alternative**: The standard approach is to either (a) force every concept into the framework, destroying the framework's clarity, or (b) silently drop what doesn't fit, creating hidden gaps. The residuals table is a third option — keep the framework clean and make the gaps explicit.

**Rule of thumb**: Any structured model should have a section titled "What's Not Here and Why." If you can't write one, you haven't thought hard enough about what you excluded.
