What do we gain from investing time and money in college?
Beyond vocational skills and potential monetary returns, what else is there?

# 1. How to Think

**Thinking** means the habit of critical reflection, applied to real life.

Common sense refers to the ideas we absorb through experience and what society tells us — and it contains biases. These assumptions shape how we think and how we perceive the world. When we arrive at college, we approach every phenomenon by relying on our common sense and forming opinions before we have examined anything. It is as if our opinions are dropped into a chemical container made of common sense, surrounded and precipitated into sediment.

The process of thinking consists of recognizing common sense, questioning it, and re-examining it from new angles.

## Why Is College a Good Environment for This?

College is defined by being "removed from society," but this very "unreality" is its unique advantage. Students are granted a four-year privilege to step away from career planning and family, and to examine society from a distance, with a pure and critical eye.

## What Can We Use in College to Build the Capacity for Thinking?

### Professors / Mentors

Professors or mentors help us see what we could not see before, expanding our perspective and pushing students to engage with thinking in the most rigorous way. They can point out the errors in our ingrained beliefs and guide us to reflect on exciting new discoveries. Their role is to train the precision, patience, responsibility, and flexibility of our minds.

### Other Students

In class, we use precise language to construct and challenge ideas. In the dorm, we talk deep into the night, discussing every topic under the sun. The former builds consensus; the latter overturns it.

## The Value of Thinking

Thinking helps us live with greater alertness, greater freedom, and greater wholeness.

## The Ideal Outcome

We can walk into society carrying questions, such as: How do I apply... in my life?

# 2. What to Think About

We can think about questions that concern the public good, thereby serving the common interest. But there is another, more particular object of thought: the self. This kind of thinking serves only personal interest, and its ultimate goal is to build a self.

# 3. The Self as the Object of Thought — Building a Self

## The General Method of Building a Self

Building a self — or, better put, building the substance of a self — means forming a complete individual made up of morality, intellect, perception, and emotion.

Everyone is born with a self, but its substance has yet to be built. Living in the world, experience is the raw material for building that substance. We feel grief, joy, compassion, and other emotions inwardly, and we process and integrate the ideas we receive through our innate capacity for thought — forming a bridge that connects thought to inner feeling, and a bridge that connects ideals to reality.

## The Role of College in Building a Self

**College provides effective tools.** In college, the books we read, the ideas we hear, and the art and creative works we encounter will push you to question every prior assumption about who you are (assumptions that society's common sense instilled in you, rather than anything your own thinking arrived at).

## What I Should Do...

- Be ready at any moment to overturn all previously accepted beliefs about who I am
- Actively take in ideas and think through them
- Once I understand what kind of person I am, seriously consider "what is truly worth pursuing," and be bold in discovering new ideals and new pursuits
  Ask yourself two questions:
  - What is a good life?
  - How do I go about having that kind of life?
