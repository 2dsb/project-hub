# Domain Formation

Domains form through two modes:
1. Coalescing around a core technology
2. Constructed from a family of phenomena

The domain housing the core technology in mode 1 and the family of phenomena in mode 2 are called the "parent domain."

# Domain Life Cycle

## Birth

Initially, a domain has not yet formed — it exists only as a kind of toolbox containing loosely structured technologies and practices.

At this stage, the new domain develops through the gradual accumulation of experiential intuition and the steady enrichment of the toolbox (evolving through understanding and practice).

The economy at this point takes a conservative stance. When necessary, it may mix the "new domain" with the parent domain, creating a "hybrid domain."

## Adolescence

The hallmark of a technological body's adolescence is the **emergence of viable technology**.

- When the technology is still in its birth phase, viable technology does not yet exist or has not yet appeared (referring to technology capable of major commercial application, or possessing the potential to build future technologies).
- The process of forming such technology encounters obstacles, requiring labs or companies to invest effort and a measure of luck.

When the obstacles in development are resolved and viable technology emerges, it triggers:
- The emergence of small enterprises attempting to commercialize the technological body, leading to technological improvements.
- A flood of investment, which may lead to a bubble.

## Maturity

Characteristics of a technological body in its maturity phase:
- The market shifts from frenzy to calm.
- Small enterprises either die out or become large corporations.
- The new domain profoundly influences the economy in its own way.

## Old Age

- Few important technologies emerge.
- Some domains are replaced by new ones; most persist, serving humanity and becoming internalized into daily life.

# Breaking the Cycle

## Variation of the Technological Body

When **key technologies undergo fundamental change** or **the application domain shifts**, variation occurs.

This kind of variation often occurs in mature technological bodies. On one hand, it forces followers of the technology to change their perception of it; on the other hand, the fundamental principles of the technology remain unchanged.

## Throwing Off Secondary Domains

For example, from the parent domain of computing and communication, sub-domains such as the internet and information technology form, with these sub-domains varying across time and place.
