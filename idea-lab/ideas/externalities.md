An externality: the uncompensated impact of one person's actions on the well-being of a bystander.
Positive externality: the impact is beneficial.
Negative externality: the impact is harmful.

![[IMG_0163.jpeg]]How to address negative externalities: taxation, **internalizing the externality** (altering incentives so that people take into account the external effects of their actions).
![[IMG_0164.jpeg]]How to address positive externalities: subsidies, internalizing the externality.

## Examples
Technology spillover effects.

# Public Policies for Externalities
1. Command-and-control policies: regulation.
2. Market-based policies: corrective taxes and subsidies / tradable pollution permits.

# Private Solutions to Externalities
1. Moral codes and social sanctions.
2. Charitable behavior.
3. Relying on the self-interest of the relevant parties to solve the externality problem, such as integrating different types of businesses together, or having the interested parties enter into contracts.

## Coase Theorem
If private parties can bargain over the allocation of resources at no cost, they can solve the problem of externalities on their own.
(Regardless of the initial distribution of rights, the interested parties can always reach an agreement in which everyone's situation can improve and the outcome is efficient.)

The problem lies in **transaction costs** (the costs that parties incur in the process of reaching and adhering to an agreement).
