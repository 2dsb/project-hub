# Economic Factors

## The Competition Between Research and Teaching

Academia views undergraduate teaching as a burden and a distraction. After World War II, as part of the space race with the Soviet Union, the United States poured massive funding into scientific research, and universities readily accepted this shift, reorienting their focus toward academic research.

As technology advanced, research outcomes became privatized. Universities exchanged research results for greater revenue, and this profit model further incentivized schools to increase research investment and recruit star professors skilled at bringing in grants — professors who were even relieved of teaching responsibilities.

## The Professor Absence Phenomenon

Tenured professors are replaced by contingent faculty, reducing costs while lowering teaching quality:

1. Adjunct instructors, though inexperienced, are extremely diligent yet overworked, leading to high turnover.
2. Temporary staff teach introductory-level courses that tenured professors avoid, yet these foundational courses have a profound impact on incoming college students.
3. Activities are substituted by teaching assistants, or in some cases, there is no interaction at all.

# Non-Economic Factors

## Professor Mindset

Since the 1970s, an oversupply of PhDs has provided universities with an abundant talent pool, while hiring standards have steadily risen. The number of academic journals and university publications has exploded, forcing professors to publish more and more articles.

Within the professoriate, there is a consensus: publish or perish — only research achievements are recognized. As a result, professors allocate their time to colleagues, graduate students, and academic conferences rather than to undergraduate education.

# Are Better Scholars Also Better Teachers?

Not at all — in fact, the two are contradictory. The logic of rigorous academic research directly undermines the capacities required for teaching:

- Good teachers use accessible, everyday language, turning abstract concepts into concrete examples and making dry material vivid — something broadly intelligible to the public. Specialized academic jargon, by contrast, is incomprehensible to ordinary people.
- Good teachers bring life into the classroom and draw connections across disciplines, whereas academia is highly specialized, with each person confined to a narrow domain of expertise.
- Good teachers have distinctive personalities, while scholars convey objective facts through abstract, impersonal language.
