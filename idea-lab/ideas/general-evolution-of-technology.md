> Technology self-organizes, self-creates, and indeed has life — but as an organism in the "coral reef" sense.

# Combination

The different ways technologies can be combined rises exponentially as the number of technologies increases.

# Demand

Before a technology is created, there may not be a statable need for it, but its emergence always has a trigger, called an "**opportunity niche**."

Opportunity niches arise from two levels:
- Human needs: humans have needs for shelter, nourishment, health, clothing, housing, etc., which relate to the stage of social development.
- Technology itself:
	1. Each technology, by its very existence, creates an opportunity to achieve a purpose more effectively or economically.
	2. Each technology requires other supporting technologies to produce it, and those supporting technologies require their own supporting technologies.
	3. Technology itself generates indirect problems, creating demand or opportunities for solutions.

# Core Mechanism

The **core mechanism** here refers to the mechanism of technology evolution in a general sense.

If individual technologies are viewed as nodes, technology in the general sense is a network.

Nodes in the network fall into two categories:
- Active technologies: poised for action, currently employed in the economy.
- Dead technologies: once used in technology but no longer — neither now nor in the future.

In terms of combination, active nodes provide combinatorial elements.

In terms of demand:
- On one hand, active nodes drive social development and reshape human needs.
- On the other hand, active nodes themselves generate demand.

The two collide: guided by demand, different active technologies are combined to form new technologies (prompting the birth of new nodes).

After a new technology emerges, it produces the following six effects on the network:
1. It becomes a new node.
2. It replaces components in past technologies.
3. It generates further demand, i.e., creates new opportunity niches.
4. The replaced components and their affiliated technologies shift from "active" to dead, and the opportunity niches they generated die out.
5. New elements become active as precursor components for future technologies or future elements.
6. The socio-economy adjusts to accommodate these steps.

In practice, these six effects can be grouped into five categories:
1. Effects on the network's nodes themselves.
2. Effects on node attributes (active/dead).
3. Effects on opportunity niches.
4. Effects on "combination."
5. Effects on the external economy.

All effects occur in parallel.

## Cascading Destruction and Trends

Cascading destruction: A new technology not only causes the collapse of the technology it replaces, but also the collapse of the demand that depended on the replaced technology, the collapse of subordinate opportunity niches, and the collapse of the technologies that occupied those niches — its impact is cascading and catastrophic.

Trends: A new technology establishes new opportunity niches, waiting for even newer technologies to occupy them, and those newer technologies in turn generate still newer opportunity niches... becoming a trend.

# Comparing Technology Evolution and Biological Evolution

Commonality: Both involve combination.

Difference: The fundamental mode of evolution for technology is combination, while for biology it is mutation.
