# Human Provides Structural Skeleton; AI Checks Completeness and Maps Linkages

**Insight**: The optimal human-AI division for knowledge structuring is not "human does thinking, AI does formatting." It's: human provides the structural skeleton (where each concept lives in the framework and why), AI cross-references against an external checklist and maps the detailed connections. Each does what the other is bad at.

**The Ch5 collaboration pattern**:
- Human: "SVM is mathematical at its core. It embeds into the (Q, A) framework as an algorithm. α-sparsity is a further constraint on its execution process."
- AI: "Checked against 116-concept list. You're missing: kernel trick (belongs in math layer, embeds in SVM execution), φ(x) (math layer, conceptually linked to kernel), template matching (conceptual layer explanation, kernel = math layer implementation), kernel machine drawbacks (defer to §5.11)."
- Human: decides what to add, skip, or modify.

**Why this split works**: Humans are good at structural insight (what something *is* and *where it belongs*) but bad at exhaustive enumeration. AI is good at exhaustive enumeration but bad at structural insight — its pattern-matching produces plausible but sometimes wrong classifications. The split gives each side the task it can do reliably.

**Contrast with the original knowledge-reconnection workflow** (Phase 1: human outlines architecture → Phase 2: AI assembles definitions): The original was about *producing a polished doc*. This refinement is about *building a mental model collaboratively*. The difference: in the original, AI does heavy lifting on prose; here, AI's primary role is completeness verification.
