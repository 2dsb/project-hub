# Teach Application Scope

**One sentence**: The `/teach` skill fits learning goals in a clear hierarchy — project-oriented learning (strongest), pure learning (medium), exam preparation (weakest, because it cannot guarantee full coverage of the exam syllabus) — so use it first for projects, and pair it with textbook self-study when preparing for exams.

## The scope

| Learning goal | Fit | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Project-oriented** | Strongest | Building something gives teach a concrete target — the fit is self-evident, no further justification needed |
| **Pure learning** | Medium | Works well for open-ended knowledge acquisition |
| **Exam preparation** | Weakest | Teach cannot achieve full coverage of the exam syllabus |

## Why exam prep is the weakest fit

Exam prep demands **total coverage**: every knowledge point on the exam syllabus (考纲) must be touched at least once. Teach is efficient but selective — it cannot guarantee nothing is missed. Full coverage is exactly the requirement teach is least able to guarantee. The failure mode is not "teach is bad," it's "teach is not built for completeness."

## How to maximize /teach

Use `/teach` primarily for **project-oriented** learning, with **pure learning** as the secondary use case. These are the two goals where teach's strengths (efficiency, targeted depth, guided-by-doing) align with what the goal demands.

## The hybrid strategy for exams

For exam preparation, do not rely on teach alone. Use a two-stage hybrid:

1. **Teach pass** — use `/teach` on the exam syllabus the same way you would for any other track — a normal, full run-through of the syllabus content.
2. **Textbook pass (gap-filling)** — study the textbook yourself to find and fill what teach left out (查漏补缺). This is what guarantees full coverage.

Teach provides the skeleton; the textbook fills the holes. The syllabus is the coverage baseline, and the textbook is the reference that catches teach's omissions — the same logic as the teach coverage check, applied to exam prep.
