# How Does Technological Innovation Happen?

The word "technology" can refer to both technology in the singular sense and technology in the plural sense.

When discussing innovation, we must consider both:
- Technology in the singular sense
- Technology in the plural sense

# (Singular) Technological Innovation

**Standard engineering**: **the process of creating a new version (also a technology) of a particular existing technology, using already-familiar combinatorial methods and structures.**

Standard engineering is also called "**design**."

Taking engineers as an example, the trigger for technological innovation is some kind of demand: adapting to the physical environment, improving efficacy, swapping in better components and materials, changes in market demand, and so on. The engineer "designs" a new technology to meet the demand.

(From the hierarchical structure of technology discussed earlier,)

**At the conceptual level, the engineer determines the overall concept design based on the demand, then completes the core integrated assembly design, followed by the subordinate sub-assembly designs.**

**At the physical level, the engineer must physically realize the conceptual design.**

The above process constitutes the basic steps of **standard engineering.**

## Standard Engineering from a "Problem-Solving" Perspective

From a problem-solving perspective, the demand is the "big problem." To solve the big problem, the engineer carries out an overall concept design and breaks the big problem into smaller problems. Specifically:

- How should the core integrated component "fit" the demand? (This can recurse: how should the sub-assembly fit the core component's demands?...)
- How can the core integrated components that meet requirements work together harmoniously?
- If the process involves communication issues and conflicts of interest in human collaboration, how should these be handled?
- When venturing into unknown territory, experimentation, iterative trial-and-error, and debugging are needed.
- ...

## Standard Engineering from a "Language" Perspective

Domains can be viewed as languages (and different domains are, of course, different languages).

In everyday life, we unconsciously generate ideas, then use a particular language to choose words and form sentences to express our views.

The basic steps of standard engineering can be understood analogously: for a given demand, the engineer first generates an "intention" (idea), and (spontaneously) seeks out a suitable domain (language), selecting technologies within it to combine (choosing words and forming sentences) to achieve the goal.

## The Difficulty Spectrum of Standard Engineering

From simple to difficult:

- Traditional projects that apply conventions and standard components
- Projects requiring practice or experimentation
- Projects with hard-to-break boundaries and special challenges

As noted above, *venturing into unknown territory* and *human factors* can significantly increase difficulty.

# (Plural) Technological Innovation

## Sample + Selection

An engineer selects suitable technologies and combines them to form a new version.

A single person's "way of combining" may be similar across designs, but when there are many engineers, vastly different combinatorial approaches emerge — leading to an immense variety of combinations.

If a technology is used enough times, it becomes a standard module, an element for constructing new technologies.

Doesn't this resemble "Darwinism"?

### Similarities with Darwinism

Good technologies get reused and become standardized (a positive feedback loop: the more a technology is reused, the more prevalent it becomes, and the easier it is to be reused again), while bad technologies fade into obscurity after solving their specific problems.

### Differences from Darwinism

- Biological evolution has a random direction and takes place over long intervals.
- The direction of technological evolution is triggered by "demand" and is artificially assembled by humans.
