Some cognitive biases affect everyone. I record them here as a reminder.

*I only read what confirms my views. Whatever comes to mind first must be the most important. Whatever I say must be right.*

# Confirmation Bias

People tend to surround themselves with those who share their views and read articles that confirm their beliefs — because it feels comfortable.
Countermeasure: Reverse the writing process. First, gather all relevant information, especially perspectives with strong opinions and insights, then form your own view.

# Feature Presence Effect (Availability Heuristic)

We tend to overestimate the importance of information that is most psychologically available to us, leaning toward the most accessible information even when it is not the most relevant.

# Ambiguity

When dealing with complex matters, the brain automatically blurs logic in pursuit of coherence, which can lead to logical errors.
Solution: Write it down. Argue in consistent, coherent language.
