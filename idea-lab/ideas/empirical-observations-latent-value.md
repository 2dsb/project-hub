# Empirical Observations Carry Latent Value — Don't Discard Them for Not Fitting Current Theory

While summarizing lesson 0002 in `teach-social-resources`, I nearly dropped the observation "family relationships don't decay when you relocate, but friendships do." My reason: it felt unimportant — just an empirical note that didn't fit the theory lesson 2 was teaching.

The agent pointed out two things I'd missed:

1. **Practical guidance, theory or not.** The observation tells me where to spend relationship-maintenance effort: not on family (stable across moves), but on friendships (which decay). A note can be actionable without being theoretical.
2. **Latent theory.** It's empirical *now*, but later lessons develop it into theory. Today's "mere observation" is tomorrow's building block.

The subtle feeling is about how I judge importance. I was scoring the observation on a single axis — "does it fit the current theory?" — and at a single point in time. But value is multi-axial (theoretical fit, practical guidance, latent potential) and time-dependent. Discarding an observation because it doesn't fit today's theory throws away value that could mature later.

This points to a note-taking rule: don't filter observations on "does it fit current theory." Record them anyway — they may guide practice immediately and theorize later.
