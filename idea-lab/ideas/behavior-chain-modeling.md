# Behavior Chains as a Person–Environment System — Modeling Near-Deterministic Influence Chains

## Core Claim

In the "person + environment" system, behavior unfolds in **near-deterministic chains**: each step to the next looks coincidental, but actually holds almost 100% given the current system state. These chains can be **modeled** — identify the objects and their interaction patterns (per `interaction-as-dictionary`) — and once modeled, **designed for**.

## Evidence — Two Observed Chains

### Chain 1: the sleep cascade (state-mismatch origin)

> 11:00 bed success → circadian 00:30 mismatch → 40 min sleepless → **physiological aversion to bed** → get up → read 1 h → book judged too boring → phone → sleep at 1:00.

The failure was not the first action (going to bed at 11:00 *succeeded*). The chain originated in a **state mismatch** — circadian clock vs. intended schedule — and cascaded from there.

### Chain 2: the money hub (design → hub → constraint → redesign)

> Hard commitment (160 yuan penalty to friend) → **money as a hub** → connected to financial gap → gap too large / awkward to explain to parents → penalty redesigned (30 yuan + bookkeeping + deferred university payment).

A designed incentive routed through a high-connectivity object (money) into a constraint (finance + parents), forcing a redesign.

## Modeling Method (per interaction-as-dictionary)

Identify **stable objects** and their **interaction patterns**; bind a name to each stable interaction cluster.

**Objects observed so far (provisional):**
- Chain 1: bed, circadian clock, sleep onset, book, phone, attention/willingness
- Chain 2: commitment, money, friend, financial gap, parents, bookkeeping, university

**Interaction patterns observed:**
- **State mismatch → ignition**: an intended state differs from a system state → the chain ignites and cascades (circadian vs. target).
- **Hub routing**: objects with many connections (money) carry a chain into other subsystems (finance → parents).
- **Value mutation**: an interaction flips an object's valence — the bed becomes aversive after failed sleep onset.
- **Low-friction wins**: at choice points the lowest-friction option is taken (phone over book — willpower is finite, `three-mental-resources`).
- **Constraint → redesign**: hitting a constraint node forces the original design to be reworked.

## Future Design (the sequel)

Once chains are modeled, design becomes possible: break a chain at its weakest link, add friction at "low-friction wins" points, pre-route around hubs, or redesign constraint nodes. This idea is the modeling; design is the follow-on.

## Open Questions

- Is there a finite list of canonical objects in the person–environment system?
- Which interaction patterns recur across *all* chains — i.e., the "grammar" of behavior chains?
- How do you verify a chain is near-deterministic vs. coincidental?
