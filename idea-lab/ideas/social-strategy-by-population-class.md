# Social Strategy by Population Class — Classify First, Then Design a Different Strategy per Class

**One sentence**: Social strategy is not one method applied to everyone — it is a **portfolio indexed by population class**; classify the population first, then design a distinct strategy per class (different goal, different maintenance mode, different tactics).

## The Move

Two steps, in order:

1. **Classify** the entire population into classes.
2. **Design** a different social strategy for each class.

The classification used here is two axes — **on-campus / off-campus** × **age / social status** — producing 5 classes.

## The Constraint the Strategy Is Built Around

The author's stated main problem: *"I have no professional ability yet, so many social relations are temporarily unusable."*

This is the condition the whole strategy is designed under, and it explains its shape. The strategy is not "meet as many people as possible." It is: build the relations that will become usable once ability arrives, and for the classes that cannot be used yet, deliberately stop at *knowing them* rather than investing in a relationship with no current outlet.

## The Five Classes

| # | Class | Axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | PKU undergraduates | on-campus · peer |
| 2 | PKU graduate students | on-campus · older |
| 3 | Professors | on-campus · senior |
| 4 | Off-campus peers | off-campus · peer |
| 5 | Society at large | off-campus · senior |

For these five, the social strategies are clearly *not* the same. Only class 1 currently has a complete strategy; the others are thin or data-lacking, and that gap is itself part of the picture.

## Class 1 · PKU Undergraduates

### Only three kinds of people are worth finding

1. **Top-k peers in some domain** — any domain counts: academics, GPA, research, projects, competitions, student work. Fine-grained qualifiers are better than broad ones: "physics department GPA top-k", "published in a top journal as a sophomore".
2. **Well-connected people.**
3. **People whose thinking is completely different from mine.**

| Kind | Importance | Maintenance effort |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Top-k peers | ★★★ | high |
| (2) Well-connected | ★★★ | high |
| (3) Different thinkers | ★★ | medium |
| Everyone else | ★ | low |

### (1) Top-k peers — feeds *learning*

- **Goal**: one-on-one guidance in the thing they are good at. If their GPA is excellent, they can compress the exploratory phase and hand over a working method directly. Every domain has a different way of learning, so the method has to come from inside that domain.
- **Maintenance**: ask questions, and keep giving them positive feedback.

### (2) Well-connected people — feeds *connection + information*

- **Goal**: use them as a **bridge** to reach more of these three kinds of people, and to learn more information.
- **Maintenance**: keep the channel active.

### (3) Different thinkers — feeds *cognitive diversity*

- **Goal**: avoid the information cocoon and the homogenization of thinking.
- **Maintenance**: a meal or coffee chat at intervals — the gap can be long.
- **Note**: partially replaceable by (1), which is why the weight is lower.

### Tactics: Chain Propagation

The mechanism that makes class 1 work is a chain, not a list of contacts.

1. **Start** — random encounters in the library, seniors, shared classes.
2. **Chain propagation** — during meals and coffee chats, plant **hooks**: recommend other people on the spot, or set up a future resource / information exchange.
3. **Feedback** — runs in two directions:
   - **Upstream**: when A gives you a connection, information, or resource, report back to A *after you use it*. If A introduces you to B, then after you talk with B, report back to A the same day.
   - **Downstream**: when you collect information that might help someone, route it to whoever it might help.
4. **Broker payoff (envisioned)** — invite two people you know from different fields to a meal or coffee chat.

Note what the single mechanism is doing: it reaches new people (2), keeps existing relationships warm (3), and is where value compounds (4). The upstream feedback rule is what keeps the chain alive — the report-back is the payment that makes A willing to make the next introduction.

## Class 3 · Professors

- **Purpose**: find a direction that interests me, and get into a lab.
- **Strategy**: approach them directly and talk — they will proactively explain their own research. For a direction that interests me: learn the fundamentals quickly, then email the professor. Alternatively, ask students in the group first, then contact the professor.

Note this is a single-purpose class: the relationship is instrumental to *research entry*, not maintained as a general connection.

## Classes 2, 4, 5 · Meet-Only for Now

- **Class 2 (PKU grad students)** and **class 5 (society at large)**: can be met, but currently limited to *knowing them*; information can be obtained. Because ability is insufficient, there is no possibility of further use — **for now**. Data is lacking.
- **Class 4 (off-campus peers)**: not many met yet. Data is lacking.

## Why the Classification Is the Point

Without the split, the only advice available is "socialize more", which is unusable — it doesn't say who to spend effort on, how to maintain them, or when to stop. The classification converts that into a per-class decision: what the goal is, what it feeds, how to hold it, and — for classes 2/4/5 — an explicit *"do not invest yet."*

The framework's current value is that the gaps are **visible**. Three of the five classes are marked data-lacking, and one has a single-purpose strategy. A general intention to network would hide all of that behind a feeling of activity.

## Open Questions

- **What unlocks classes 2, 4, 5?** The stated blocker is ability. Is the trigger a specific milestone (a paper, a lab position, a project), or is it continuous and gradual?
- **Does (3) really deserve a lower weight?** The argument for downgrading it is that (1) partly replaces it. But (1) selects for people who excel within their domain — which is exactly the selection pressure that produces homogenized thinking. Whether (1) can substitute for (3) is worth re-examining.
- **What does the broker move actually pay?** Introducing two people from different fields is a service to both, but the return to the introducer is unspecified.
