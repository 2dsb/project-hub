# Coffee Chat: The Three Questions

## The Pattern

Three questions, asked in this order:

```
1. Work    — "Tell me about your work."          → what they do
2. Best    — "What are you best at?"             → where their edge is
3. Talent  — "What's your strongest talent?"     → what comes naturally to them
```

## The Order Matters

The three are an escalation, not a list. Each one earns the next:

| # | Question | What it reaches | Why it's answerable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce your work | The **public** layer — role, project, domain | Easiest: work is what they talk about all day |
| 2 | What you're best at | The **peak** layer — where they exceed peers | Flattering, needs self-assessment, still professional |
| 3 | Your strongest talent | The **innate** layer — the source behind the peak | Rarely asked, so the answer is unpolished and personal |

Q1 is safe and supplies raw material. Q2 asks them to rank themselves and surfaces the edge. Q3 asks for the thing *behind* the edge. Do not open with Q3 — it reads as an interrogation when it hasn't been paid for by the two softer questions.

## Why Question 3 Is the Point

Q1 and Q2 describe **what someone has**. Q3 tries to locate **what generates it** — the underlying capability that produced both the work and the edge. The aim is *absorption*, not information.

Two things make it strong in practice:

- **Almost nobody asks it.** People are asked about their work constantly and about their strengths occasionally. Being asked to name a *talent* is memorable, and it can't be answered on autopilot — you get a real answer instead of a rehearsed one.
- **It's structurally flattering.** Calling something a talent treats them as having a distinctive capacity rather than a job. That's a stronger compliment than praising the work itself.

## The Ceiling

Talent does not transfer by being described. Three limits worth knowing before you ask:

1. **They may not know.** Talents are often invisible from the inside. The person may never have had to articulate it.
2. **Label ≠ mechanism.** "I'm good with people" is not installable. Chase it — ask *how*, then ask what causes that, the way [[attribution-of-achievement]] drills down.
3. **Some of it isn't transferable at all.** Temperament and accumulated history aren't a technique. What actually transfers is usually the **pointer** — what to practice, what to pay attention to.

Net: high ceiling on insight, lower ceiling on transfer. It reliably tells you where to look; it doesn't hand you the ability.

## How to Deliver It

The failure mode is instrumental — "what's your talent" asked flatly sounds like sizing someone up as a resource. The problem is framing, not the question. It works when genuine admiration is already on the table, which is why it sits third, after two rounds of demonstrated interest. It fails as an opening move.
