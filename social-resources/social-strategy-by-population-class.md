# Social Strategy by Population Class

## The Rule

Social strategy is not one method applied to everyone. **Classify the population first, then design a different strategy for each class.**

Without the classification, the only available advice is "socialize more" — which doesn't say who to spend on, how to maintain them, or when to stop.

## Step 1 — Classify

Two axes: **on-campus / off-campus** × **age / social status**.

| # | Class | Axis | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PKU undergraduates | on-campus · peer | full strategy below |
| 2 | PKU graduate students | on-campus · older | meet-only, data lacking |
| 3 | Professors | on-campus · senior | single-purpose (research entry) |
| 4 | Off-campus peers | off-campus · peer | meet-only, data lacking |
| 5 | Society at large | off-campus · senior | meet-only, data lacking |

## Step 2 — Assign Each Class a Strategy

For every class, fix four things: **goal · what it feeds · maintenance mode · how much effort it earns.**

### Class 1 · PKU Undergraduates — the full case

**Only three kinds of people are worth finding:**

1. **Top-k peers in some domain.** Any domain counts — academics, GPA, research, projects, competitions, student work. Prefer fine-grained qualifiers: *"physics department GPA top-k"*, *"published in a top journal as a sophomore"*.
2. **Well-connected people.**
3. **People whose thinking is completely different from mine.**

| Kind | Importance | Goal | What it feeds | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (1) Top-k peers | ★★★ | One-on-one guidance in what they're good at | Learning | Ask questions; keep giving positive feedback |
| (2) Well-connected | ★★★ | Use as a bridge to reach more of (1)(2)(3) | Connection + information | Keep the channel active |
| (3) Different thinkers | ★★ | Avoid the information cocoon | Cognitive diversity | Meal / coffee chat at intervals — long gaps fine |
| Everyone else | ★ | — | — | Low priority |

**On (1):** every domain has a different way of learning, so the method has to come from inside that domain. Someone with an excellent GPA can compress the exploratory phase and hand you a working method directly.

**On (3):** partially replaceable by (1), which is why the weight is lower — but worth re-examining, since (1) selects for people who excel *within* their domain, which is exactly the pressure that produces homogenized thinking.

### The Chain Propagation Loop (Class 1)

**1. Start.** Random encounters in the library · seniors · shared classes.

**2. Propagate.** During meals and coffee chats, plant **hooks** — recommend other people on the spot, or set up a future resource / information exchange.

**3. Feed back — the protocol.** Runs in two directions:

| Direction | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Upstream** | A gives you a connection, information, or resource | Report back to A **after you use it**. If A introduces you to B, talk to B, then report back to A **the same day**. |
| **Downstream** | You collect information that might help someone | Route it to whoever it might help |

The upstream rule is what keeps the chain alive — the report-back is the payment that makes A willing to make the next introduction.

**4. Broker (envisioned).** Invite two people you know from different fields to a meal or coffee chat.

**Why one loop does all of it:** the same mechanism reaches new people (2), keeps existing relationships warm (3), and is where value compounds (4).

### Class 3 · Professors

- **Purpose**: find a direction that interests me, and get into a lab.
- **Steps**: approach them directly and talk — they will proactively explain their own research → for a direction that interests me, learn the fundamentals quickly → email the professor. Alternative route: ask students in the group first, then contact the professor.

Note this is a single-purpose class: the relationship is instrumental to research entry, not maintained as a general connection.

### Classes 2, 4, 5 · Meet-Only — Do Not Invest Yet

- **Class 2 (PKU grad students)** and **class 5 (society at large)**: can be met; currently limited to *knowing them*. Information can be obtained. Further use is not possible **for now**.
- **Class 4 (off-campus peers)**: not many met yet.

Data lacking for all three. The classification's value here is making that gap **visible** rather than hiding it behind a feeling of activity.

## The Constraint Underneath

*"I have no professional ability yet, so many social relations are temporarily unusable."*

This is the condition the whole strategy is designed under. It is not "meet as many people as possible" — it is: build the relations that become usable once ability arrives, and for the classes that can't be used yet, stop at knowing them.
