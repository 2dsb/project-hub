# The Advice-Seeking Method

## The Pattern

A simple, structured way to ask for advice — designed to draw out not only what you know to ask about, but also what you don't.

```
1. Open: "Could you give me a bit of advice?"
2. (Prepare several angles of your own ahead of time.) Go through them one at a
   time, and let the other person advise on each.
3. Once they've spoken on every angle you had: "I can't think of any more
   questions — is there any other advice?"
4. After they finish: "So that should be all of it, right? Anything else?"
```

## Why It Works

- **Prepare angles before, not during.** You don't blank under pressure or forget what you meant to ask — the conversation has a pre-set shape.
- **One angle at a time** gives each topic a full answer rather than a rushed bundle.
- **The "any other advice?" turn is the key move.** It flips the frame from *your* questions to *their* knowledge — surfacing advice you wouldn't have known to ask for.
- **The final "that's it? anything else?"** is a cheap completeness check that squeezes out the last bits before the conversation ends.
